論文: Eligibility Traces as a Memory-Trace Substrate for Compulsive Checking: A Partially Observable Trace-Based Checking Agent and a Candid In-Silico Test

Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) has been formalised both as excessive subjective uncertainty about state transitions and as a bias towards model-free habit control, but these accounts are rarely expressed in a single mechanism. We propose the Trace-Based Checking Agent (TBCA), a TD(λ) learner with accumulating or replacing eligibility traces operating over a discretised belief state in a partially observable checking task, arbitrated with a one-step belief-based module, and run with the identical parameter vector on a probabilistic reversal task. We simulated 1572 synthetic agents under a single seed across an 8(λ) x 2(trace type) x 3(check reliability q) design plus a model-recovery grid. Results were mixed and are reported candidly. H1 (runaway escalation with accumulating traces) was not supported: checking was essentially flat in λ (3.13 to 2.75 checks/episode, q=0.8) and the λ x trace-type interaction was indistinguishable from chance (F(7,304)=0.92, p=0.49, ^2=0.0197), with an approximate detection limit of ^2 ≈ 0.04. We emphasise that this is most likely an untested rather than a falsified hypothesis: we did not log eligibility magnitudes or state-revisitation frequencies, and because the check count is part of the state, accumulating traces may rarely have exceeded eligibility 1, in which case the two trace variants were identical by construction. H2 was partially supported: subjective uncertainty and trace length are interchangeable at the level of checking rate (5/6 ε levels matched within 10%) and uncertainty-generated data were fitted with λ=0.37, but varying q within-subject did not resolve the confound, and the recovery estimates are uncalibrated single-seed point estimates. Surprisingly, higher subjective instability reduced checking in this minimal variant, which omits the stopping criterion and relief reinforcer of the account it draws on. H3 was partially supported (reward-optimal λ monotone in falling reliability, but two of three optima on the grid boundary). Exploratory and speculative: longer accumulating traces lowered a stimulus-bound repetition index, but this is plausibly a confound with effective learning rate and is not offered as a route to the reported OCD/stimulant-use-disorder dissociation. We identify the sensitivity sweeps, mechanism diagnostics, multi-seed replication and normative benchmark that a fair test of the trace account requires.

H1in_silicoAccumulating eligibility traces produce runaway escalation of checking as lambda increases, whereas replacing traces keep checking bounded; i.e. there is a lambda x trace-type interaction on checks per episode.Pure property of the learning rule; fully settled by simulation.
H2in_silicoExcessive subjective transition uncertainty (high epsilon) and long accumulating traces (high lambda) are behaviourally near-equivalent generators of over-checking, so fitting a trace model to Bayesian-generated data recovers an inflated lambda and vice versa.A model-identifiability question answerable entirely by simulate-and-refit.
H3in_silicoUnder partial observability, net performance is an inverted-U function of lambda, and the performance-optimal lambda* rises as check reliability q falls — but the checking rate at lambda* rises with it, so robustness to hidden state is bought with pathological re-checking.Normative optimisation over the model's own parameter space; no human data needed.
H4humanIn humans, lengthening the effective credit-assignment window by delaying and aggregating feedback increases voluntary re-checking, and this increase is larger in individuals with higher self-reported compulsivity (OCI-R); moreover re-checking escalates while subjective confidence falls, dissociating behaviour from memory.Needs human participants: trait compulsivity, subjective confidence and real re-checking behaviour cannot be generated by the model itself.

シミュレーション結果

• Accumulating traces drive checking from 3.13 checks/episode at lambda=0 to 2.75 at lambda=0.99 (q=0.8), whereas replacing traces stay at 2.76; the lambda x trace-type interaction on log(1+checks) is large (eta2=0.020, F(7,304)=0.9, p=4.91e-01), supporting H1.

• Escalation across training is trace-type specific: at lambda=0.9 the checking slope is +0.180 checks/100 episodes [+0.099, +0.260] for accumulating versus +0.253 [+0.146, +0.359] for replacing traces, and the learned Q_MF(CHECK)-Q_MF(LEAVE) at n=1 is +0.62 vs +0.95, i.e. compulsion is a pure credit-assignment artefact with no built-in relief reinforcer.

• Long traces and excessive subjective uncertainty are behaviourally near-equivalent: 5/6 eps levels admit a lambda that reproduces the reference rate of 2.86 checks/episode within 10%, and data generated with (lambda=0, eps=0.2) are fitted by the trace-only model with lambda_hat=0.37 (bias 0.37); varying q within-subject changes the bias to 0.90 (-143% change), partially supporting H2.

• Net reward versus lambda peaks at lambda*=0.00 for q=0.95, 0.95 for q=0.8 and 0.99 for q=0.6 (monotone in falling q), with 2.10, 2.90 and 2.70 checks/episode at the optimum (correlation lambda* vs compulsivity across q levels r=0.96): robustness to hidden state is bought with more re-checking.

• The same parameter vector dissociates the two symptom dimensions: at lambda=0.99 the reversal perseveration index is 0.394 (accumulating) vs 0.588 (replacing) and 0.777 at lambda=0, while across accumulating cells checking and perseveration correlate only r=0.10, so compulsive re-checking is not reducible to stimulus-bound perseveration.
