# Scout report — 強迫症の記憶トレースモデルの発展

**Queries:** OCD, eligibility trace, modeling, obsessive-compulsive disorder computational model, eligibility trace reinforcement learning credit assignment

The literature contains two largely separate strands. On the clinical-computational side, OCD has been formalized either as excessive uncertainty about state transitions within a Bayesian-brain framework, which reproduces doubt, checking, repetition and over-reliance on habit (fradkin2020searching), or as a shift in the arbitration between model-based and model-free control, with a habit-learning bias shared across compulsivity disorders and linked to reduced caudate and medial OFC grey matter (voon2014disorders; radua2009voxelwise). Hierarchical Bayesian fits of probabilistic reversal learning show that OCD and stimulant use disorder dissociate on perseveration parameters and are differentially modulated by D2/3 agents (kanen2019computational). On the machine-learning/neuroscience side, eligibility traces are a well-characterized mechanism for credit assignment over time, with accumulating vs. replacing variants, off-policy generalizations, and robustness to hidden state (singh1996reinforcement; precup2000eligibility; kaelbling1996reinforcement), and electrophysiological evidence in rats constrains dopaminergic learning to long-lasting traces with low learning rates (pan2005dopamine). What is missing is an explicit bridge: no retrieved work estimates eligibility-trace parameters in OCD patients or uses trace dynamics as the memory-trace substrate for obsessive repetition, despite traces being the natural formal link between model-free and Monte-Carlo/model-based-like credit assignment (precup2000eligibility; moerland2023modelbased).

## Research gaps

- **P1** (gap) Can eligibility-trace parameters (trace decay lambda, trace type, learning rate alpha) be identified from OCD patients' sequential choice data, and does OCD correspond to abnormally persistent (or abnormally short) traces? — _fradkin2020searching, kanen2019computational, pan2005dopamine, singh1996reinforcement_
- **P2** (contradiction) Do accumulating versus replacing eligibility traces produce dissociable signatures of repetitive and stimulus-bound behaviour, and can a replacing/accumulating trace model reproduce the finding that stimulus-bound perseveration is increased in stimulant use disorder but decreased in OCD? — _singh1996reinforcement, kanen2019computational, voon2014disorders_
- **P3** (open_question) Are the Bayesian 'excessive state-transition uncertainty' account and the 'model-free habit bias' account of OCD mutually exclusive, or can both be generated by a single trace-based agent (e.g., a TD(lambda)/off-policy-trace agent) in which trace and model-learning parameters trade off? — _fradkin2020searching, voon2014disorders, precup2000eligibility, moerland2023modelbased_
- **P4** (gap) Under partial observability — where the agent cannot verify whether its own action was executed — do longer eligibility traces trade off between compensating for hidden state and producing pathological repeated re-checking? — _precup2000eligibility, kaelbling1996reinforcement, fradkin2020searching_
- **P5** (extension) Do D2/3 agonists and antagonists (pramipexole, amisulpride) shift estimated trace-decay and learning-rate parameters in OCD patients in the direction predicted by dopaminergic eligibility-trace physiology? — _pan2005dopamine, kanen2019computational_
- **P6** (gap) Do individual trace-based model parameters predict structural abnormalities (caudate/lenticular enlargement, dorsomedial frontal/ACC reduction) and response to cognitive-behavioural therapy in OCD? — _voon2014disorders, radua2009voxelwise, clark2003cognitivebehavioral, bates2015fitting_
